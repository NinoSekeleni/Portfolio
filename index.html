    Visa · Paris 2024 Olympic GamesA Moment for More

    Role
    Creative Lead, campaign and digital
    Year
    2024
    Market
    South Africa
    Audience
    Millennials and Gen Z
    Channels
    TikTok, Meta, in-game media, experiential, print

    The Olympic Games are one of Visa's biggest sponsorship properties, and the Paris 2024 brief in South Africa was to build brand love and preference while getting Millennials and Gen Z to use their Visa cards in store, online and across borders.

    Our research surfaced three truths about this audience. They want to live life on their own terms, they value experiences and presence over possessions, and above all they crave a life well lived. One message could never land the same way with everyone, so we built a platform flexible enough to feel personal to each person: A Moment for More, the idea that every payment moment is a push towards more, whatever more means to you.

    The platform ran across TikTok, Meta and in-game media with sprinter Akani Simbine as its hero and nine influencers across three tiers amplifying it, giving fans a chance to win R10,000 and meet him. We extended it into A Moment for Fun, an activation where Gen Z could give Akani their best South African shout-out or race a 100-metre sprint for a chance to meet him, and into issuer campaigns such as Absa's, where cardholders who spent R5,000 on their Visa card during the Games were entered into a draw to meet Akani.

    In the same year I led the digital and print campaigns for Visa's sponsorships of both the Olympics and the ICC Men's T20 World Cup, with one consistent message across social and web.

    That work has grown into a wider partnership with Visa's marketing services team, for whom I lead creative on the campaigns Visa runs with its partner banks across Africa. In Visa's own report for the first quarter of its 2026 financial year, Investec's Pep Guardiola FIFA campaign lifted cross-border transactions by 11% and spend per active card by 5%, and CBZ's AFCON campaign in Zimbabwe grew Visa payment volume by 28% in a month. Together with FNB's It Starts Here and Chipper Cash's AFCON launch in Nigeria, those four campaigns brought Visa $2.4M in revenue in a single quarter.

    Visa × FNB · FIFA World Cup 2026It Starts Here

    Role
    Creative Lead, campaign and art direction
    Launch
    July 2025
    Segments
    Personal, Private and Commercial
    Channels
    Film, radio, digital OOH, social, direct mail, web

    Visa briefed Migration to help FNB become the first South African bank to own the FIFA World Cup 2026 conversation, a full year before the tournament kicks off.

    The answer was It Starts Here, a three-phase campaign built on the belief that every big moment begins somewhere close to home, on a township pitch, at a spaza shop or in a small business getting ready for the tournament. We wrote it for FNB's Personal, Private and Commercial customers and carried it through a 60-second manifesto film, radio, digital out-of-home, social, direct mail and web.

    The first phase, Tap & Win, gave FNB Visa cardholders who shopped between July and September 2025 the chance to win a trip to the World Cup or a match-day kit, and a live activation at Mall of Africa brought the campaign to fans in person. At launch, FNB's chief marketing officer described FNB as the first bank to bring the 2026 FIFA World Cup to South Africans, with three customers set to travel to the tournament. Visa's own report on the quarter recorded growth in cards in force and active cards, more than 32% growth in new-card spend, and lower dormancy across FNB's Visa base.

    DP World Lions CricketThe Pride of Jozi

    Role
    Creative Lead, brand and campaigns
    Seasons

    Discipline

    Partner

    Migration has been the strategic and creative partner to Lions Cricket for five seasons, and for four of them I have led the brand and campaign creative for the men's and women's teams, building The Pride of Jozi across brand, content, commercial and match-day communication.

    The Pride of Jozi joins the idea of a lions' pride with the city's pride in its team. We turned it into a full brand system for the DP World Lions, with a professional brand book, stationery and merchandise, and into a match-day content engine that covers fixtures, results, player milestones, sponsor moments such as Acronis' Catch of the Match, and events like the season launch at DP World Wanderers Stadium. The DP World Lions manifesto film puts the players in front of the camera to tell fans, in their own words, what the Pride of Jozi stands for.

    The results are measured independently. Nielsen Sports valued the sponsor exposure on the Lions' own social channels at R3.5 million in advertising value between August 2022 and January 2024, from 3,996 branded posts that earned 23.2 million impressions. The corner logo I built into every post template was Nielsen's single most valuable sponsor placement, with 6.25 million impressions. Between May 2021 and May 2024 the club's Instagram following more than doubled, from 7,200 to 16,100.

    Each season carries its own theme, and on the field the Lions won seven trophies in 2023/24. Off it, the work helped the club win Sport Organisation of the Year at the Hollard Sport Industry Awards in both 2023 and 2024.

    Ikigai Kofi Hys · Brand identityWhere hustle meets harmony

    Role
    Brand identity, art direction and web design
    Year
    2026
    Place
    Alexandra, Johannesburg
    Scope
    Positioning, identity, guidelines, packaging, signage, uniforms, menu, website and social
    AI
    Every application visualised in the space before production

    Ikigai Kofi Hys is a new coffee house in Alexandra that joins the Japanese idea of ikigai, a reason for being, with "kofi hys", the township's own word for a coffee house. It needed a brand that could feel tranquil and vibrant at once, a place to slow down that still carries the rhythm of the street outside.

    I built the identity around that meeting of two worlds, which the guidelines call Afro-Zen. The wordmark is custom lettering drawn with the rhythm of a Japanese reed pen, its floating i-dots a nod to lanterns and breath, and it sits on a palette of forest, beige, charcoal and white, with Cormorant Garamond and Inter setting the voice. An ensō circle, a single brushstroke and an ink splash give the brand three quiet motifs, and "Pour. Sit. Breathe." gives it a line of its own.

    I then carried the system through every touchpoint the shop needs, from takeaway cups, menu boards and the printed menu to an A-frame, a window decal, a hero wall, aprons, a loyalty card, the website and its social pages. I used AI to visualise each application in the space, so the owners could see the whole brand working together before anything went to print.

    I also designed and built the shop's website, which leads with venue hire alongside the menu so the space can earn beyond the counter. Its enquiry form turns a table booking or venue request into a ready-to-send WhatsApp message, so requests arrive where the team already talks to customers and nothing is stored on a server. The full menu and venue rates are one tap away as downloads, and local search markup helps people find it as a coffee shop in Alexandra.

    Lwazi Loyiso Qhama Dekeda · AI-led book coverFormed to Take the Hill Country

    Role
    Cover design and art direction
    Year
    2026
    Format
    A5 trade paperback, front, 12 mm spine and back
    Process
    Mood board site, directions site, final artwork site, print-ready PDF
    AI
    Concept imagery, mock-ups and the three client sites

    Formed to Take the Hill Country weaves the author's story with four generations of a South African family, and his brief was simple: hand him the book rather than a style, because he would rather be surprised by something true than comforted by something expected.

    This is my most recent AI-led project. I used AI to explore each creative world quickly, generating mood imagery, concept renders and realistic book mock-ups, and built three interactive presentation sites so the author could review every stage on his phone or laptop. The judgement stayed human: every direction starts from a line in the manuscript.

    I started with a round-one mood board of four creative worlds, each with its own imagery, tones and typography, which the author could star and annotate as he went. That narrowed into three complete directions, each beginning from a different sentence in the manuscript. The Relief is a contour pressed into bone-coloured stock, Nec Tamen Consumebatur returns the burning bush from his grandfather's granite headstone to the high ground, and Out of the Rock sends a single shoot up through split stone. Every direction was carried across the front, spine and back, and shown as flat artwork, at thumbnail size and as a physical book.

    The author chose Nec Tamen Consumebatur, Latin for "yet it was not consumed". I resolved it into a cover built like an old hymnbook, with cream stock, an ornamental double frame and a burning-bush seal at its centre, then delivered print-ready artwork tested on a retail shelf and down to a 56-pixel thumbnail.
